Structured Data: Ordinal & Nominal
Page 19
• Ranked by order (Ordinal)
• Credit Ratings
AAA, AA+, A, BBB, BB, etc. Ordinal scale indicating creditworthiness. Used for 
bond pricing and risk assessment.
• Analyst Recommendations
 Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, Sell, Strong Sell. Consensus ratings aggregate expert 
opinions into actionable signals.
• No rank (Nominal)
• Industry Classifications
 GICS sectors, SIC codes, NAICS. Categorical data for sector rotation and peer 
comparison strategies.
• ESG Scores
 Environmental, Social, Governance ratings (A-F or 0-100 scale). Growing 
importance in sustainable investing.

Ordinal Data: Usage Considerations
Page 20
Special handling for ranked data
• Non-Linear Distances
The difference between AAA and AA is not the same as BB and B. Statistical methods 
must account for ordinal nature.
• Encoding Strategies
Label encoding (1, 2, 3...), one-hot encoding, or learned embeddings. Choice affects 
model performance in ML applications.
• Consensus Challenges
Different rating agencies may disagree. Aggregating ordinal data requires careful 
methodology (median vs mean).
• Time Variation
Ratings change over time. Tracking upgrades and downgrades can signal investment 
opportunities.

Unstructured Data: Text
Page 23
The richest source of qualitative information
News Articles & Press Releases
Breaking news drives market reactions. Event studies analyze price movements around 
announcements. Sentiment analysis extracts tone.
Earnings Call Transcripts
Management tone, word choice, Q&A dynamics. NLP techniques detect deception, 
confidence, and forward guidance.
SEC Filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K)
Risk factors, MD&A sections reveal management concerns. Text analysis tracks changes 
across filings for early warning signals.
Social Media & Forums
Twitter, Reddit (WallStreetBets), StockTwits. Retail sentiment, meme stock detection, 
crowd wisdom or madness.

Data Integration Challenges
Page 33
Data Quality & Cleaning
Missing values, outliers, errors. Unstructured data is noisy. Text has typos, images have 
poor quality, audio has background noise.
Scale & Computational Cost
Processing millions of documents, images, audio files requires significant infrastructure. 
Cloud computing essential.
Feature Engineering
Converting unstructured data to structured features. Embeddings, sentiment scores, 
image descriptors must be meaningful for prediction.
Legal & Privacy Concerns
Alternative data may have restrictions. Personal data requires compliance with GDPR, 
CCPA. Material non-public information risks

MAS AI/ML Governance in Finance
34
MAS FEAT Principles of AI Governance
1. Fairness
•AI and data-driven decisions should be accurate, unbiased, and equitable.
•Customers with similar profiles should be treated consistently.
•Institutions should put in place measures to identify and mitigate bias in 
data, models, and processes.
2. Ethics
AI should be used in a responsible and transparent way.
Decisions must align with the firm’s ethical standards and societal norms.
Institutions are accountable for outcomes from AI use — they cannot “blame 
the algorithm.”

MAS AI/ML Governance in Finace
35
MAS FEAT Principles of AI Governance
3. Accountability
Financial institutions remain responsible for the outcomes of their AI models.
Clear governance structures must be in place, including board oversight and 
internal accountability mechanisms.
Human-in-the-loop principles should ensure important decisions are 
explainable and reviewable.
4. Transparency
Institutions should explain to customers, regulators, and stakeholders how 
AI-driven decisions are made.
Transparency should be proportionate to the impact (e.g., higher when 
decisions affect credit, insurance, or investments).
Proper documentation of models, data sources, and processes is essential.

Data Governance framework
Page 37
• Data Governance is the framework of rules, roles, processes, and 
technologies that ensures data is managed as a trusted, secure, and 
valuable asset across an organization.
• Key Objectives
• Data Quality – accuracy, completeness, timeliness, consistency.
• Data Security & Privacy – compliance with laws (GDPR, PDPA).
• Standardisation – common data definitions, taxonomies, and 
metadata.
• Accountability – clear ownership of data assets (data stewards, 
custodians).
• Value Creation – enable better analytics, decision-making, and 
innovation.

How to implement projects in AI/ML? 
• Set your objectives (pick small wins first) 
• Decide whether to do inhouse or with outside vendors. What are the pros and 
cons of this? 
• Decide on the time line and the budget. 
• Decide what data you need. Do you already have them or need to source for 
some of them. Are the data confidential and need to satisfy PDPA or other 
regulations? How should you regulate the data? 
• Data pre-processing and cleaning to make it useful for ML algorithms
• Train the AI/ML model. Analyse it for its implications. Run UAT on the model.
• Finally put into operations. Finetune it. 

Conclusion
Page 41
• Role of AI/ML in Business Finance – Machine learning and analytics uncover data 
patterns for predictive and generative tasks across trading, customer 
prospecting/ segmentation, news monitoring, credit scoring, and regulatory

well-governed data and adherence to principles (fairness, ethics, accountability, 
transparency) to build trust and ensure responsible model deployment.
